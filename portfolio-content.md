## Work cases
Eyebrow: Work cases
Heading: Four problems, and what changed

### 1. AI Product Command Center → an 8-agent team
Tag: 2026 AI Impact Award
In a nutshell: I turned my own product workload into an orchestrated team of Claude agents, then rebuilt it in two months.
The challenge: One product owner carrying backlogs, releases, competitive tracking and stakeholder updates for global teams in three regions.
What I built: A Command Center combining calendar, blockers, release health, competitive intelligence and a living roadmap, kept current by scheduled automation. Then a Chief of Staff agent orchestrating 7 specialists, each with a charter and hand-offs, token-efficient context and a UI for visibility.
Key results: **12–15 hours saved per week.** **2x throughput** on specs and decisions. Recognized with the company's **2026 AI Impact Award**.

### 2. AI for manufacturing quality
In a nutshell: bringing AI insights, agents and predictive intelligence to the InfinityQS and PQ Systems quality (SPC) products.
The challenge: Quality data spread across product families had to become useful, connected AI capabilities for specialty manufacturers.
What I did: Own delivery of AI data insights, workspaces, agents, workflows and predictive intelligence through the Advantive ONE intelligence platform.
Key results: AI agents, workflows and predictive intelligence **shipping to customers** as part of **Advantive ONE**, launched publicly in **April 2026** as a unified intelligence layer across operational, financial and quality data.

### 3. Scaling a portfolio through acquisitions
In a nutshell: helped the portfolio grow from 8 to 16 core brands by making acquired products and teams work as one.
The challenge: Acquired legacy products arrived with fragmented practices, and new product owners needed to get productive fast.
What I did: Product management lead for 3 acquisitions; wrote the company's integration playbook; moved acquired teams to Scrum and products from sustainment to modernization; built a Product Owner Bootcamp.
Key results: **8 → 16 core brands**. **20% faster** product owner ramp-up. Supported a **$219M** billings-based revenue base.

### 4. A privacy-first hall pass for schools
Tag: Independent product · Pre-launch
In a nutshell: a teacher's idea, taken from concept to a deployed web app, so students can leave class without interrupting it.
The challenge: Every hall pass pulled a teacher out of instruction, paper logs failed during lockdowns and drills, and schools are wary of vendors holding student data.
What I built: A student sign-out kiosk, live staff board, reports and lockdown snapshot, built with Claude, Next.js, Supabase and Vercel. Student names are encrypted in the school's own browser, and the interface adapts by grade band, from picture-and-audio for early readers to a fast flow for high schoolers.
Key results: **Phase 1 built and tested**. A design where the service **stores no readable student data** and holds none of the keys. Launch and go-to-market underway.

## AI builds
Eyebrow: AI builds
Heading: My 8-agent AI team
Intro: I set direction. A Chief of Staff agent routes work to seven specialists and assembles what comes back.

Diagram:
- Me: direction & decisions
- Chief of Staff: Orchestrates, routes hand-offs, keeps context lean
- Specialists:
  - Customer SME: Voice of strategic customers
  - Platform SME: Platform knowledge and fit
  - Competitive Intel: Market and competitor analysis
  - Feature Writer: Drafts specs and features
  - Grooming: Backlog refinement
  - Program Mgmt: Releases, risks and dependencies
  - Comms: Stakeholder updates

### How I build with AI
- **Charters and hand-offs:** Every agent has one job, a written charter and a defined hand-off, so work moves cleanly between them.
- **The right context:** Agents are grounded in a curated knowledge base, and each works from the context it needs and nothing more. Accuracy goes up and token cost comes down.
- **Connected to live data:** Custom MCP connectors I built, plus prebuilt ones, link agents to roadmap, delivery and CRM tools, so they work from live data instead of stale copies.
- **Guardrails first:** Written instructions and guardrails define what each agent can access, what it can change, and when it hands a decision back to me.
- **Scheduled, not prompted:** Skills and scheduled tasks keep the system current without someone having to ask.
- **Visible by design:** A UI shows what each agent is doing, so the output is easy to trust and check.
